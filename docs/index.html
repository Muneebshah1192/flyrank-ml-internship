Machine Learning Capstone · Research Paper

Which content deserves attention first?

Refresh Opportunity Scoring for search content: a study of whether machine learning can improve a practical review queue built from observable search-performance and content signals.

FlyRank ML Internship Warehouse v20260703 April 1 – June 30, 2026 104,301 modeling records Client-grouped holdout
Abstract

Search-content teams often have more pages to review than editorial capacity allows. This study asks whether observed search-performance signals can be turned into a useful refresh-prioritization queue. Using the FlyRank internship warehouse, the analysis compares a transparent rule-based score with Logistic Regression and Random Forest, while keeping later outcome information outside the model inputs. The evaluation uses a client-grouped holdout and Precision@50 to reflect the quality of the first 50 review recommendations. The rule baseline achieved a measured Precision@50 of 0.80, compared with 0.68 for Random Forest and 0.56 for Logistic Regression. In this experiment, the simpler rule provided stronger observed top-of-queue prioritization than the tested machine-learning models.

01 · Research question

Start with the decision, not the model

The question

Which published content items should be prioritized for refresh review based on observed search-performance signals and content characteristics?

The decision

When review capacity is limited, which pages should an editorial or SEO team inspect earlier than the rest?

Intended use. The output is a review queue. A high score means “look here earlier,” not “rewrite this page automatically.”

Daily performance supplies GSC impressions, clicks, CTR-related inputs and position-related measurements. The content dimension supplies contextual information such as search volume, competition, content type, word count, backlinks, creation date, update date and optimization date.

Why this time window?

April provides the historical reference period. June provides a later observed outcome. Requiring both periods prevents a newly observed item from being interpreted as a “decline” simply because it lacked the earlier measurement.

Missing metadata was represented with explicit missingness indicators before imputation. Client and content identifiers were used for grouping and joins, not prediction.

Observed target

is_declining = 1 when June impressions were at least 20% below April impressions.

In the 104,301-record modeling population, 77,431 records were labeled declining and 26,870 were not.

This is an observed proxy outcome. It is not a measure of Google's internal ranking state.

Why Precision@50?

The operational question is not “How accurate is every row?” It is “How useful are the first few recommendations?” Precision@50 therefore evaluates the concentration of observed declines at the top of the queue.

Core result. The experiment does not support adding ML complexity merely because the task is an ML capstone. Under this particular design, the simple rule was more effective at the top of the queue.

Baseline composition

The winning rule uses three transparent components:

  • 40% historical impression opportunity
  • 35% low-CTR opportunity
  • 25% freshness opportunity

What this does not mean

It does not mean rules will always beat machine learning. It means that the two tested models, the selected features, the chosen outcome and this client-grouped holdout did not outperform this baseline.

05 · Findings

What a content team can reasonably take from the study

01

High visibility + strong decline is worth inspecting

Items with meaningful historical visibility and substantial later decline are useful candidates for earlier review.

02

Simple scoring can be operationally strong

In this experiment, a transparent score was easier to explain and also produced the strongest measured top-50 precision.

03

Top-of-queue quality matters

Precision@50 keeps the evaluation aligned with the real use case: limited editorial capacity and a need to prioritize.

04

A ranking is not a diagnosis

A measured decline can have many explanations. The queue tells a reviewer where to look, not what caused the movement.

06 · Ranked recommendations

Turn the score into a human review workflow

1
Review high-priority items first. Confirm that the observed decline is meaningful and that the content remains strategically relevant.
2
Investigate low-CTR opportunity. Review title, snippet, intent match and SERP context before changing content.
3
Check freshness. Confirm whether the page is outdated, incomplete, or misaligned with current user needs.
4
Allow “monitor” as an action. Not every measured movement justifies a rewrite.

Reason codes

STRONG_DECLINE LOW_CTR_OPPORTUNITY OLDER_CONTENT MIXED_SIGNAL

Human review checklist

Check strategic importance, current demand, technical health, external events, search intent, competing pages and whether refresh is preferable to monitoring, consolidation or no action.

07 · Limitations

Keep the claims smaller than the dataset

LimitationImplication
Observed proxy outcomeThe label measures impression movement, not Google's internal ranking logic.
One selected time windowResults may change with seasonality, demand or future conditions.
One client-grouped holdoutPrecision@50 is not a guarantee of future performance.
Uneven metadata coverageSome content fields have meaningful missingness.
Observational designThe study cannot prove that refreshing a page causes recovery.
Claim discipline: The findings are best described as observed, measured, directional and decision-support. They should not be presented as causal evidence about Google's ranking algorithm or as proof that a refresh will recover traffic.
